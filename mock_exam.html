1(b) Wet Steam Properties at 8 bar 9 Marks

Given wet steam at $P = 8\text{ bar}$ and $u = 2450\text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate:

  1. Dryness fraction ($x$) [3 Marks]
  2. Specific volume ($v$) [3 Marks]
  3. Specific enthalpy ($h$) [3 Marks]
Hint: At $8\text{ bar}$: $u_f = 720$, $u_g = 2577$, $v_g = 0.2404$, $h_f = 721$, $h_{fg} = 2048$.

Step 1: Dryness Fraction ($x$)
$$u = u_f + x(u_g - u_f) \implies 2450 = 720 + x(2577 - 720) \implies \mathbf{x = 0.9316}$$

Step 2: Specific Volume ($v$)
$$v = x \cdot v_g = 0.9316 \times 0.2404 = \mathbf{0.2239\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}}$$

Step 3: Specific Enthalpy ($h$)
$$h = h_f + x \cdot h_{fg} = 721 + 0.9316(2048) = \mathbf{2628.92\text{ kJ/kg}}$$

2(a) Energy Interactions in a System 9 Marks

A closed system undergoes energy transfers: $W_1 = 0.96\text{ MJ}$ (in), $W_2 = 202\text{ kJ}$ (out), $Q_1 = 2.16\text{ MJ}$ (out), $Q_2 = 46.9\text{ kJ}$ (in), $Q_3 = 35.9\text{ kNm}$ (out). Calculate:

  1. Total amount of work ($W_{\text{net}}$) in kJ. [4 Marks]
  2. Value of $Q_4$ if internal energy increases by $120\text{ kJ}$. [5 Marks]
Hint: Sign convention: Heat IN (+), Heat OUT (-), Work OUT (+), Work IN (-).

Step 1: Net Work ($W_{\text{net}}$)
$$W_1 = -960\text{ kJ}, \quad W_2 = +202\text{ kJ} \implies W_{\text{net}} = -960 + 202 = \mathbf{-758\text{ kJ}}$$

Step 2: Calculate $Q_4$
$$Q_{\text{net}} = -2160 + 46.9 - 35.9 + Q_4 = -2149 + Q_4$$ $$\Delta U = Q_{\text{net}} - W_{\text{net}} \implies 120 = (-2149 + Q_4) - (-758) \implies \mathbf{Q_4 = +1511\text{ kJ}}$$

3(a) Turbine Power & Fluid Flow 10 Marks

Fluid flows steadily in a turbine at $\dot{m} = 4.17\text{ kg/s}$, $P_1 = 6.5\text{ bar}$, $v_1 = 0.25\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$, $A_1 = 0.35\text{ m}^2$. Leaves at $P_2 = 5.2\text{ bar}$, $v_2 = 0.40\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. Given $u_2 - u_1 = -800\text{ kJ/kg}$, heat loss $\dot{Q} = -255\text{ kW}$, $C_2 \approx 0$. Determine:

  1. Inlet velocity ($C_1$). [3 Marks]
  2. Power output ($\dot{W}$). [7 Marks]
Hint: $C_1 = \frac{\dot{m} v_1}{A_1}$. Express enthalpy change as $h_2 - h_1 = (u_2 - u_1) + (P_2 v_2 - P_1 v_1)$.

Step 1: Inlet Velocity ($C_1$)
$$C_1 = \frac{4.17 \times 0.25}{0.35} = \mathbf{2.98\text{ m/s}}$$

Step 2: Power Output ($\dot{W}$)
$$h_2 - h_1 = -800 + [(520 \times 0.40) - (650 \times 0.25)] = -754.5\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$\dot{Q} - \dot{W} = \dot{m}(h_2 - h_1) \implies -255 - \dot{W} = 4.17 \times (-754.5) \implies \mathbf{\dot{W} = +2891.27\text{ kW}}$$

2(a) Isothermal Oxygen Compression 8 Marks

$0.5\text{ kg}$ of oxygen ($M = 32\text{ kg/kmol}$) is compressed isothermally from $1.013\text{ bar}$ and $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($298\text{ K}$) to $5.5\text{ bar}$. ($R_u = 8.3145\text{ kJ/kmol}\cdot\text{K}$). Calculate:

  1. Specific gas constant ($R$). [2 Marks]
  2. Work input ($W$). [4 Marks]
  3. Heat transfer ($Q$). [2 Marks]
Hint: $R = R_u / M$. For isothermal process of an ideal gas, $\Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W$.

Step 1: Specific Gas Constant ($R$)
$$R = \frac{8.3145}{32} = \mathbf{0.2598\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}}$$

Step 2: Work Input ($W$)
$$W = m R T \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = 0.5 \times 0.2598 \times 298 \times \ln\left(\frac{1.013}{5.5}\right) = 38.71 \times (-1.6917) = \mathbf{-65.48\text{ kJ}}$$

Step 3: Heat Transfer ($Q$)
$$Q = W = \mathbf{-65.48\text{ kJ (Heat Rejected)}}$$

3(a) Compressor Steady Flow Analysis 8 Marks

Air flows through a steady compressor at $\dot{m} = 30\text{ kg/s}$. Inlet properties: $h_1 = 1744.4\text{ kJ/kg}$, $C_1 = 15\text{ m/s}$, $v_1 = 0.34\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. Outlet properties: $h_2 = 3556\text{ kJ/kg}$, $C_2 = 35\text{ m/s}$. Heat loss to atmosphere is $\dot{Q} = -30\text{ kW}$. Calculate:

  1. Power required ($\dot{W}$) in kW. [5 Marks]
  2. Compressor's inlet cross-sectional area ($A_1$). [3 Marks]
Hint: Apply SFEE: $\dot{Q} - \dot{W} = \dot{m}\left[(h_2 - h_1) + \frac{C_2^2 - C_1^2}{2000}\right]$. Continuity: $\dot{m} = \frac{A_1 C_1}{v_1}$.

Step 1: Power Required ($\dot{W}$)
$$\Delta h = 3556 - 1744.4 = 1811.6\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$\Delta KE = \frac{35^2 - 15^2}{2000} = \frac{1225 - 225}{2000} = 0.5\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$-30 - \dot{W} = 30 \times (1811.6 + 0.5) = 30 \times 1812.1 = 54363$$ $$\dot{W} = -30 - 54363 = \mathbf{-54,393\text{ kW (or } 54.39\text{ MW input)}}$$

Step 2: Inlet Area ($A_1$)
$$A_1 = \frac{\dot{m} v_1}{C_1} = \frac{30 \times 0.34}{15} = \mathbf{0.68\text{ m}^2}$$

4(a) Carnot Heat Engine Performance 6 Marks

A Carnot heat engine operates between temperatures of $999\text{ K}$ and $376\text{ K}$. It receives heat at a rate of $810\text{ kJ/s}$ from the hot reservoir. Calculate:

  1. Thermal efficiency ($\eta_{th}$). [3 Marks]
  2. The rate of heat rejected to the cold reservoir ($\dot{Q}_L$). [3 Marks]
Hint: $\eta_{th, Carnot} = 1 - \frac{T_L}{T_H}$. $\dot{Q}_L = \dot{Q}_H (1 - \eta_{th}) = \dot{Q}_H \left(\frac{T_L}{T_H}\right)$.

Step 1: Thermal Efficiency
$$\eta_{th} = 1 - \frac{376}{999} = 1 - 0.3764 = 0.6236 \implies \mathbf{62.36\%}$$

Step 2: Heat Rejected Rate ($\dot{Q}_L$)
$$\dot{Q}_L = \dot{Q}_H \times \left(\frac{T_L}{T_H}\right) = 810 \times \left(\frac{376}{999}\right) = \mathbf{304.86\text{ kW}}$$

SET 3: Final Examination (Sesi I 2023/2024)
1(a) Constant Temperature Ideal Gas Cooling 12 Marks

$0.55\text{ kg}$ of a perfect gas ($M = 44.01\text{ kg/kmol}$) is contained in a volume of $V_1 = 0.10\text{ m}^3$ at a pressure of $P_1 = 4\text{ bar}$ ($400\text{ kPa}$). It is cooled at constant temperature until its pressure drops to $P_2 = 1.2\text{ bar}$ ($120\text{ kPa}$). Calculate:

  1. Initial temperature ($T_1$). [6 Marks]
  2. Final volume ($V_2$). [6 Marks]
Hint: $R = R_u / M = 8.3144 / 44.01 = 0.1889\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. Use $P_1 V_1 = m R T_1$ and $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$.

Step 1: Initial Temperature ($T_1$)
$$T_1 = \frac{P_1 V_1}{m R} = \frac{400 \times 0.10}{0.55 \times 0.1889} = \frac{40}{0.1039} = \mathbf{384.98\text{ K (or } 111.98^\circ\text{C)}}$$

Step 2: Final Volume ($V_2$)
$$P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies 400 \times 0.10 = 120 \times V_2 \implies V_2 = \frac{40}{120} = \mathbf{0.333\text{ m}^3}$$

2(a) Work Done on Closed System ($CO_2$) 11 Marks

$1\text{ kg}$ of $CO_2$ gas in a closed system undergoes a reversible constant pressure process (Isobaric). During this process, internal energy decreases by $42\text{ kJ}$ ($\Delta U = -42\text{ kJ}$). Given $c_p = 840\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$ and $c_v = 600\text{ J/kg}\cdot^\circ\text{C}$, calculate the work done ($W$).

Hint: $\Delta U = m c_v (T_2 - T_1)$. Find $\Delta T$, then calculate $Q = m c_p (T_2 - T_1)$ and use First Law $W = Q - \Delta U$.

Step 1: Temperature Change ($\Delta T$)
$$\Delta U = m c_v (T_2 - T_1) \implies -42,000\text{ J} = 1 \times 600 \times \Delta T \implies \Delta T = -70^\circ\text{C}$$

Step 2: Heat Transfer ($Q$)
$$Q = m c_p \Delta T = 1 \times 840 \times (-70) = -58,800\text{ J} = -58.8\text{ kJ}$$

Step 3: Work Done ($W$)
$$Q - W = \Delta U \implies -58.8 - W = -42 \implies W = -58.8 + 42 = \mathbf{-16.8\text{ kJ (Work Input)}}$$

3(a) Carnot Cycle Power Plant 8 Marks

A steam power plant operates on a Carnot cycle between $P_{\text{boiler}} = 40\text{ bar}$ ($T_1 = 250.4^\circ\text{C} = 523.4\text{ K}$, $h_{fg1} = 1714\text{ kJ/kg}$) and $P_{\text{condenser}} = 0.04\text{ bar}$ ($T_2 = 29^\circ\text{C} = 302\text{ K}$). Calculate:

  1. Thermal efficiency ($\eta_{th, Carnot}$). [4 Marks]
  2. Net work output per kg ($W_{net}$). [4 Marks]
Hint: $\eta_{Carnot} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}$. $Q_{in} = h_{fg1}$. $W_{net} = \eta \cdot Q_{in}$.

Step 1: Thermal Efficiency
$$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{302}{523.4} = 1 - 0.577 = 0.423 \implies \mathbf{42.3\%}$$

Step 2: Net Work Output
$$W_{net} = \eta \times Q_{in} = 0.423 \times 1714 = \mathbf{725.02\text{ kJ/kg}}$$

SET 4: Model Final Examination (Comprehensive Practice)
1(a) Superheated Steam Property Interpolation 12 Marks

Steam at $P = 30\text{ bar}$ has a specific enthalpy of $h = 3080\text{ kJ/kg}$. Using superheated steam tables ($30\text{ bar}$: $300^\circ\text{C} \rightarrow h=2995$, $350^\circ\text{C} \rightarrow h=3117$, $v=0.0905\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$), determine:

  1. Temperature of the steam ($T$). [6 Marks]
  2. Specific volume ($v$) by interpolation. [6 Marks]
Hint: Single linear interpolation between $300^\circ\text{C}$ and $350^\circ\text{C}$.

Step 1: Calculate Temperature ($T$)
$$\frac{T - 300}{350 - 300} = \frac{3080 - 2995}{3117 - 2995} \implies \frac{T - 300}{50} = \frac{85}{122} = 0.6967$$ $$T = 300 + (50 \times 0.6967) = \mathbf{334.84^\circ\text{C}}$$

Step 2: Calculate Specific Volume ($v$)
At $300^\circ\text{C}, v_1 = 0.0811\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. At $350^\circ\text{C}, v_2 = 0.0905\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$.
$$v = 0.0811 + 0.6967 \times (0.0905 - 0.0811) = 0.0811 + 0.00655 = \mathbf{0.08765\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}}$$

2(a) Adiabatic Expansion in Piston-Cylinder 12 Marks

Air ($m = 0.2\text{ kg}$, $\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 0.287\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$) expands adiabatically from $P_1 = 12\text{ bar}$, $T_1 = 600\text{ K}$ to a final volume $V_2 = 3 V_1$. Calculate:

  1. Initial volume ($V_1$). [3 Marks]
  2. Final pressure ($P_2$). [3 Marks]
  3. Final temperature ($T_2$). [3 Marks]
  4. Work done ($W$). [3 Marks]
Hint: $P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma$ and $T_2 = T_1 (V_1/V_2)^{\gamma-1}$.

Step 1: Initial Volume ($V_1$)
$$V_1 = \frac{0.2 \times 0.287 \times 600}{1200} = \mathbf{0.0287\text{ m}^3}$$

Step 2: Final Pressure ($P_2$)
$$P_2 = P_1 \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^\gamma = 12 \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{1.4} = 12 \times 0.2149 = \mathbf{2.58\text{ bar (258 kPa)}}$$

Step 3: Final Temperature ($T_2$)
$$T_2 = 600 \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{0.4} = 600 \times 0.6444 = \mathbf{386.64\text{ K}}$$

Step 4: Work Done ($W$)
$$W = \frac{m R (T_1 - T_2)}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{0.2 \times 0.287 \times (600 - 386.64)}{1.4 - 1} = \frac{12.248}{0.4} = \mathbf{30.62\text{ kJ}}$$

3(a) Insulated Steam Nozzle 10 Marks

Steam enters an insulated nozzle at $h_1 = 3038\text{ kJ/kg}$ with velocity $C_1 = 30\text{ m/s}$. Mass flow rate is $\dot{m} = 2.5\text{ kg/s}$. At exit, $h_2 = 2700\text{ kJ/kg}$ and $v_2 = 0.22\text{ m}^3/\text{kg}$. Calculate:

  1. Exit velocity ($C_2$). [5 Marks]
  2. Exit area ($A_2$). [5 Marks]
Hint: SFEE: $h_1 + \frac{C_1^2}{2000} = h_2 + \frac{C_2^2}{2000}$. Continuity: $\dot{m} = \frac{A_2 C_2}{v_2}$.

Step 1: Exit Velocity ($C_2$)
$$3038 + \frac{30^2}{2000} = 2700 + \frac{C_2^2}{2000} \implies 338.45 = \frac{C_2^2}{2000} \implies \mathbf{C_2 = 822.74\text{ m/s}}$$

Step 2: Exit Area ($A_2$)
$$A_2 = \frac{2.5 \times 0.22}{822.74} = \mathbf{0.000668\text{ m}^2\text{ (or } 6.68\text{ cm}^2\text{)}}$$

4(a) Rankine Cycle Power Plant 15 Marks

A Rankine cycle operates between $P_{\text{boiler}} = 30\text{ bar}$ ($h_1 = 2804\text{ kJ/kg}$, $s_1 = 6.187\text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$) and $P_{\text{condenser}} = 0.05\text{ bar}$ ($h_f = 138$, $h_{fg} = 2423$, $s_f = 0.476$, $s_{fg} = 7.918$, $v_f = 0.001005$). Calculate:

  1. Dryness fraction at turbine exit ($x_2$). [4 Marks]
  2. Turbine work ($W_T$) and Pump work ($W_P$). [5 Marks]
  3. Thermal efficiency ($\eta_{\text{Rankine}}$). [6 Marks]
Hint: $s_1 = s_2 = s_f + x_2 s_{fg}$. $W_P = v_f (P_4 - P_3)$.

Step 1: Dryness Fraction ($x_2$)
$$6.187 = 0.476 + 7.918 x_2 \implies 5.711 = 7.918 x_2 \implies \mathbf{x_2 = 0.7212}$$

Step 2: Turbine & Pump Work
$$h_2 = 138 + 0.7212(2423) = 138 + 1747.47 = 1885.47\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$W_T = 2804 - 1885.47 = \mathbf{918.53\text{ kJ/kg}}$$ $$W_P = 0.001005 \times (3000 - 5) = \mathbf{3.01\text{ kJ/kg}} \implies h_4 = 138 + 3.01 = 141.01\text{ kJ/kg}$$

Step 3: Thermal Efficiency ($\eta_{\text{Rankine}}$)
$$W_{net} = 918.53 - 3.01 = 915.52\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$Q_{in} = h_1 - h_4 = 2804 - 141.01 = 2662.99\text{ kJ/kg}$$ $$\eta_{\text{Rankine}} = \frac{915.52}{2662.99} = 0.3438 \implies \mathbf{34.38\%}$$
